# Horizons in Cosmology

> [!summary] Core idea
> Particle and event horizons are causal scales determined by integrals of expansion history; the Hubble radius $c/H$ is a distinct kinematic scale and is generally not a causal horizon.

> [!caution] Orientation status
> This is a compact orientation note and has not yet been source-audited. The epistemic label describes the standing of the underlying physics in its stated regime; it does not certify every sentence or equation in this note.

## Mathematical handle

$\displaystyle \chi_{\rm particle}(t)=c\int_0^t\frac{dt'}{a(t')},\qquad
\chi_{\rm event}(t)=c\int_t^\infty\frac{dt'}{a(t')},\qquad
R_H(t)=\frac{c}{H(t)}$

The first two are comoving causal horizons (when the event-horizon integral converges); multiplying by $a(t)$ gives their proper distances. The Hubble radius is local in time and need not coincide with either causal horizon.
