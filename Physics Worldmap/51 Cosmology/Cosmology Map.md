# Cosmology Map

> [!abstract]
> The origin, contents, geometry, expansion, structure, and fate of the observable universe.

## Questions that organize the field

- What are dark matter and dark energy?
- What set the primordial conditions and fluctuations?
- How did cosmic structure and the first luminous objects form?
- Is the standard cosmological model complete and internally consistent?

## Working principles

- Cosmological inference is model-dependent and combines geometry, growth, and astrophysical systematics.
- Homogeneity and isotropy are large-scale statistical approximations.
- Distinguish horizons, observable limits, and the entire universe.
- Cross-check parameters with probes having different degeneracies.

## Canonical mathematical handles

- **Friedmann:** $H^2=(8\pi G/3)\rho-kc^2/a^2+\Lambda c^2/3$ — Expansion follows cosmic contents and curvature.
- **Acceleration:** $\ddot a/a=-4\pi G(\rho+3p/c^2)/3+\Lambda c^2/3$ — Pressure gravitates and can accelerate or decelerate expansion.
- **Continuity:** $\dot\rho+3H(\rho+p/c^2)=0$ — Homogeneous cosmic energy evolves with expansion.
- **Perturbation growth:** $\ddot\delta+2H\dot\delta-4\pi G\rho_m\delta=0$ — Matter overdensities grow gravitationally in a basic regime.

## Concept atlas

| Concept | Level | Status | One-line orientation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| [[Cosmological Principle]] | introductory | established | On sufficiently large scales the universe is modeled as statistically homogeneous and isotropic. |
| [[FLRW Spacetime]] | intermediate | established | The Friedmann-Lemaitre-Robertson-Walker metric is the most general spacetime consistent with exact homogeneity and isotropy. |
| [[Cosmic Expansion]] | introductory | established | Comoving separations grow with the scale factor while locally bound systems need not expand. |
| [[Hubble Lemaitre Law]] | introductory | established | At low redshift, recession velocity inferred in the homogeneous model is proportional to distance. |
| [[Friedmann Equations]] | intermediate | established | General relativity reduces cosmic dynamics to equations for the scale factor sourced by mean density and pressure. |
| [[Cosmological Redshift]] | introductory | established | Expansion stretches photon wavelengths in proportion to the scale factor. |
| [[Cosmological Distances]] | intermediate | established | Comoving, angular-diameter, luminosity, and lookback distances encode geometry and expansion differently. |
| [[Horizons in Cosmology]] | advanced | established | Particle and event horizons are expansion-history integrals; the Hubble radius is a distinct kinematic scale and generally not a causal horizon. |
| [[Thermal History of the Universe]] | intermediate | established | Expansion and interactions drive epochs of equilibrium, freeze-out, nucleosynthesis, recombination, and reionization. |
| [[Big Bang Nucleosynthesis]] | advanced | established | Light-element abundances formed in the first minutes depend on baryon density, expansion, nuclear rates, and neutrino physics. |
| [[Cosmic Microwave Background]] | intermediate | established | Relic radiation from recombination carries a near-blackbody spectrum and anisotropies encoding primordial fluctuations and cosmic contents. |
| [[Recombination and Last Scattering]] | advanced | established | Electrons and nuclei formed neutral atoms, allowing photons to free-stream from a finite visibility window. |
| [[Primordial Perturbations]] | advanced | established | Small early curvature and matter fluctuations seed later cosmic structure. |
| [[Inflation]] | advanced | active | A phase of accelerated early expansion can generate nearly scale-invariant perturbations and solve horizon and flatness puzzles, but its microphysics is unknown. |
| [[Structure Formation]] | advanced | active | Gravitational instability amplifies primordial inhomogeneities into halos, galaxies, filaments, and voids. |
| [[Matter Power Spectrum]] | advanced | established | The power spectrum quantifies variance of density modes and records primordial, dark-matter, baryonic, and expansion physics. |
| [[Baryon Acoustic Oscillations]] | advanced | established | Pre-recombination sound waves imprint a calibrated comoving scale in late-time clustering. |
| [[Gravitational Lensing in Cosmology]] | advanced | established | Weak and strong lensing map integrated mass and geometry, probing growth and dark matter. |
| [[Dark Matter in Cosmology]] | intermediate | open | Nonbaryonic gravitating matter explains cosmic structure and multiple observations, but its microscopic identity remains unknown. |
| [[Dark Energy]] | intermediate | open | Late-time accelerated expansion is consistent with a cosmological constant but could reflect a field, modified gravity, or systematics. |
| [[Cosmological Constant Problem]] | advanced | open | Observed vacuum-like energy is extraordinarily small relative to naive quantum-field estimates and not understood. |
| [[Hubble Tension]] | advanced | open | Some early- and late-universe inferences of the present expansion rate disagree beyond expected uncertainties, with cause unsettled. |
| [[Reionization]] | advanced | active | The first luminous sources ionized intergalactic hydrogen, leaving signatures in the CMB and high-redshift spectra. |
| [[Cosmic Neutrino Background]] | advanced | established | Standard cosmology predicts relic neutrinos whose gravitational effects are observed indirectly but direct detection remains unrealized. |
| [[Fate of the Universe]] | advanced | open | Long-term expansion depends on dark energy behavior, matter stability, black holes, and physics far beyond tested timescales. |
