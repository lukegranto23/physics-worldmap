# Renormalization as Compression Across Scales

The renormalization group asks which information about microscopic states remains relevant after coarse observation. Information theory asks which representation preserves predictive information under compression. The common core is a map from fine variables to coarse variables plus a criterion for what must be retained.

## Established bridge

- [[Renormalization Group]] classifies operators by how their influence changes with scale.
- [[Information Theory]] quantifies retained and discarded dependence.
- Sufficient statistics preserve all information about a specified parameter; RG coarse variables preserve long-distance observables near a fixed point.

## Non-equivalence

Generic compression has no reason to respect spatial locality, symmetry, compositionality, or a semigroup of scale changes. Those constraints are central to physical RG.

## Productive question

Can a learned bottleneck be constrained so its latent variables transform locally under symmetry and its repeated application approaches known fixed points? Test on models with exact or high-precision critical exponents before using it on unknown matter.
