# Topological Defects Across Matter and Cosmology

When an ordered medium samples different regions of an order-parameter manifold, spatial incompatibility can trap defects classified by that manifold's topology. This logic spans superfluids, magnets, liquid crystals, superconductors, and cosmological field models, but the dynamics that create defects are additional to their topological classification.

## Common mechanism

1. symmetry and order-parameter manifold;
2. domain formation during a transition—for example, a finite freeze-out correlation length in the Kibble–Zurek mechanism, which is one production route rather than a universal definition of a defect;
3. incompatible local choices;
4. homotopy class prevents smooth removal.

## Differences that matter

Cosmic expansion, gauge fields, causal horizons, thermalization, and post-transition coarsening differ sharply between laboratory and cosmological systems. Analogue experiments test mechanism and scaling, not the literal existence of cosmic defects.
