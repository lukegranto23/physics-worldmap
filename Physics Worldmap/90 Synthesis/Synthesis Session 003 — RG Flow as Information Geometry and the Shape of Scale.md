# Synthesis Session 003 — RG Flow as Information Geometry and the Shape of Scale

> [!warning] Research status
> The controlled question is whether a **specified finite-dimensional RG truncation** is approximately a metric-gradient flow for an independently defined monotone. “RG is Fisher natural-gradient descent” is not a general theorem, and this note makes no claim of novelty.

[[Synthesis Lab]] · [[Information Geometry]] · [[Renormalization Group]] · [[Synthesis Session 001 — Response-Preserving Coarse-Graining]]

## 1. Established pieces

### RG as a vector field on theory space

For couplings $g^i$ and scale $\mu$,

$$
\beta^i(g)=\mu\frac{d g^i}{d\mu}.
$$

The components depend on coordinates and renormalization scheme, although fixed points, critical exponents, and suitable observables have invariant content. A real-space blocking step usually generates infinitely many couplings; projecting back to a few parameters is an additional approximation.

### Fisher geometry for a declared statistical family

For normalized distributions $p(x|g)$,

$$
g^{F}_{ij}=\mathbb E_g\!\left[\partial_i\log p\,\partial_j\log p\right].
$$

For a Gibbs exponential family, this is a covariance matrix of the operators conjugate to the couplings. In continuum QFT, contact terms, regulators, operator mixing, and normalization must be handled before identifying such a metric with a correlator-defined metric on theory space.

### RG monotones and gradient formulae

Zamolodchikov’s two-dimensional $c$-theorem supplies a function monotone along unitary RG flow. A nonperturbative two-dimensional gradient formula has the more careful form

$$
\partial_i c=-\bigl(g_{ij}+\Delta g_{ij}+b_{ij}\bigr)\beta^j,
$$

where $b_{ij}$ is antisymmetric and $\Delta g_{ij}$ is a correction. This is not automatically the simple Fisher natural-gradient equation $\beta^i=-\left(g_F^{-1}\right)^{ij}\partial_j c$. Higher-dimensional monotonicity theorems also do not imply one universal Fisher-gradient structure.

Relative entropy contracts under a specified stochastic or quantum channel. That data-processing fact can illuminate coarse-graining, but it does not by itself identify a QFT $c$-function with a Kullback–Leibler divergence.

### Holographic scale

In controlled AdS/CFT constructions, bulk radial evolution is related to boundary scale. Information metrics and bulk geometric quantities coincide in some special calculations, but the spacetime metric, a metric on states, and a metric on couplings live on different manifolds. They must not be equated without an explicit dictionary.

## 2. The precise candidate

Choose a model and a closed truncation $g=(g^1,\ldots,g^n)$ with $n\ge2$. Let a fixed blocking rule produce an estimated RG displacement $\Delta g^i$. Independently choose:

- a normalized data distribution $p(x|g)$ and its Fisher metric $g^F_{ij}$;
- a scalar candidate monotone $\Phi(g)$ derived without using the measured $\Delta g$;
- a matching procedure that includes induced operators or quantifies truncation error.

Then compare the measured flow with

$$
v^i_{\rm NG}=-\left(g_F^{-1}\right)^{ij}\partial_j\Phi.
$$

A coordinate-invariant alignment statistic is

$$
\cos\theta=
\frac{g^F_{ij}\,\Delta g^i v^j_{\rm NG}}
{\sqrt{g^F_{ij}\Delta g^i\Delta g^j}\,
 \sqrt{g^F_{ij}v^i_{\rm NG}v^j_{\rm NG}}}.
$$

Agreement of directions is only one test; the proportionality factor, blocking-step dependence, induced-coupling residual, and monotonicity of $\Phi$ must also be reported.

## 3. What Computational Lab 15 establishes

`62 Computational Labs/15_rg_fisher_geometry.py` implements a useful pipeline check for the two-dimensional Ising model:

- Wolff sampling near the known critical coupling;
- a majority-rule block-spin transformation;
- an uncalibrated one-coupling proxy obtained as the inverse hyperbolic tangent of nearest-neighbor correlation;
- the energy-variance/Fisher response, which peaks near criticality at finite size.

It does **not** test the central natural-gradient claim. In one coupling dimension, any two nonzero directions are collinear, so “alignment” is tautological. Defining $F(K)=\int g(K)\beta(K)dK$ from the measured flow constructs a potential by design; resemblance to a peaked curve does not identify it with Zamolodchikov’s $c$-function. The one-coupling closure also discards interactions generated by blocking.

The inverse-hyperbolic-tangent relation is exact for a suitable infinite one-dimensional chain, not two-dimensional Ising near criticality. Even the sign and zero of the reported coupling-displacement proxy need not match a calibrated RG flow. The lab is therefore exploratory: it runs the sampling/blocking/response pipeline and exposes why calibrated multi-coupling inference and an independent test are required.

## 4. Decisive benchmark

1. Use at least nearest-neighbor, next-nearest-neighbor, and plaquette couplings.
2. Infer all retained couplings with uncertainty after each blocking step.
3. Measure the full covariance/Fisher matrix on held-out configurations.
4. Choose $\Phi$ from a theorem-backed or independently computed quantity, not an integral of the observed beta field.
5. Compare actual and natural-gradient directions across multiple starting points and blocking schemes.
6. Track the norm of discarded induced operators.
7. Repeat under smooth reparameterizations to confirm coordinate-invariant conclusions.

### Pre-registered failure conditions

The hypothesis is disfavored if alignment is no better than matched random or ordinary-gradient baselines, fails under modest enlargement of the coupling basis, depends strongly on blocking convention, or requires a potential reconstructed from the same flow it is meant to predict.

## 5. Holographic extension

Only after a nontrivial field-theory benchmark succeeds should one ask whether a state or coupling information metric maps to a bulk geometric quantity. A valid extension must specify:

- which boundary family is varied;
- which information metric is used;
- which bulk slice or geometric functional is compared;
- the large-$N$, coupling, and semiclassical approximations;
- a tensor-network or continuum counterexample capable of falsifying the map.

Conformal symmetry matching and the slogan “radial direction = scale” are not sufficient to prove that Fisher geodesic distance is bulk proper distance.
