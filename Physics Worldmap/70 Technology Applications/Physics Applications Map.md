# Physics Applications Map

> [!abstract]
> Where physical principles become instruments, energy systems, materials, information technologies, medicine, and engineering capability.

## Questions that organize the field

- Which physical limit constrains performance?
- Which efficiency, safety, cost, reliability, and lifecycle tradeoffs dominate?
- How does a laboratory effect become a scalable technology?

## Working principles

- Separate fundamental limits from current engineering limits.
- Include materials, manufacturing, control, environment, and economics in system boundaries.
- Quantify safety margins, failure modes, and lifecycle impacts.

## Canonical mathematical handles

- **Efficiency:** $\eta=E_{\rm useful}/E_{\rm input}$ — Performance is bounded by conservation and irreversibility.
- **Power density:** $P/V$ — Scaling often constrains cooling and materials.
- **Information capacity:** $C=B\log_2(1+\mathrm{SNR})$ — Bandwidth and noise constrain communication.

## Suggested study loop

1. Read the field questions, then explain them from memory.
2. Learn the introductory concepts and reproduce their limiting cases.
3. Derive at least one canonical equation from a variational, symmetry, or conservation principle.
4. Reproduce one landmark result computationally or from public data.
5. Audit one frontier claim: known facts, model dependence, discriminating observable, and next experiment.
