## Path A — conceptual literacy

1. [[Foundations of Physics Map]]
2. introductory concepts in every field map;
3. [[Scale Ladder]] and [[Approximation and Regime Map]];
4. one landmark experiment per field;
5. one open problem audit per month.

## Path B — undergraduate core

1. calculus, linear algebra, ODEs, vector calculus, probability;
2. mechanics, oscillations, waves, thermodynamics;
3. electromagnetism, special relativity, quantum mechanics;
4. statistical physics, continuum or modern laboratory;
5. numerical methods and uncertainty.

## Path C — theoretical graduate spine

1. analytical mechanics and mathematical methods;
2. advanced quantum mechanics and statistical mechanics;
3. classical field theory, general relativity, or many-body theory;
4. quantum field theory and renormalization;
5. specialization plus current review literature;
6. reproduce a published result.

## Path D — experimental research spine

[[Experimental Physics Map]] → electronics and control → statistics and inference → domain instrumentation → calibration and systematics → reproducible analysis → independent cross-check.

## Path E — computational research spine

[[Computational Physics Map]] → numerical analysis → structure-preserving methods → verification and validation → inverse problems and UQ → domain-scale simulation.

## Weekly recall protocol

- Day 1: reconstruct definitions and dimensions.
- Day 2: derive one result.
- Day 3: solve one limiting case.
- Day 4: connect to one experiment.
- Day 5: run or alter a simulation.
- Day 6: compare two neighboring theories.
- Day 7: write a synthesis card with a falsifier.

See [[Problem Solving Protocol]], [[Recall Question Bank]], and [[Physics Problem Bank]].
