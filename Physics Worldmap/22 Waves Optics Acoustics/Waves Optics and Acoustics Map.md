# Waves Optics and Acoustics Map

> [!abstract]
> Propagation, interference, diffraction, resonance, imaging, and information carried by disturbances.

## Questions that organize the field

- How do boundary conditions and dispersion shape propagation?
- When are ray, wave, and quantum descriptions appropriate?
- How much information can a wave field carry or resolve?
- How do nonlinearity, disorder, and topology alter transport?

## Working principles

- Linear waves superpose; sources and boundaries select modes.
- Phase is as important as amplitude.
- Dispersion links spatial and temporal structure through the dispersion relation.
- Resolution, bandwidth, loss, and noise impose coupled limits.

## Canonical mathematical handles

- **Wave equation:** $\partial_t^2\psi=c^2\nabla^2\psi$ — A nondispersive scalar wave propagates at speed c.
- **Dispersion:** $\omega=\omega(\mathbf k)$ — Phase and group velocities follow from a mode relation.
- **Helmholtz:** $(\nabla^2+k^2)\psi=0$ — Monochromatic wave structure is a boundary-value problem.
- **Fraunhofer transform:** $U(\mathbf k_\perp)\propto\mathcal F\{A(\mathbf r_\perp)\}$ — Far-field diffraction is a spatial Fourier transform.
