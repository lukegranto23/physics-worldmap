# Source and Citation Policy

## Evidence order

Prefer, in order appropriate to the claim:

1. collaboration data releases, standards bodies, observatory or laboratory documentation;
2. peer-reviewed primary papers;
3. authoritative review articles and living reference compilations;
4. graduate or advanced undergraduate textbooks;
5. pedagogical courses and reputable lecture notes;
6. tertiary explanations only for orientation.

ArXiv is essential for current work but is not itself peer review. News releases help discovery, not technical adjudication.

## Citation record

For a claim likely to change, record:

- title, authors or institution, URL or DOI;
- publication and access date;
- exact figure, table, section, or data release;
- what the source supports and what it does not;
- relevant caveats, corrections, and later updates.

## Documentation-status rule

Scientific status and note documentation are separate properties:

- `epistemic_status` describes how secure the **physics claim** is in its stated domain;
- `source_audit` describes whether this **particular note** has complete note-level citations.

The short concept, experiment, and derivation notes are deliberately marked as orientation or canonical summaries when their note-specific citation audit is pending. Do not remove that marker until the source, supported claim, scope, and caveats have actually been checked. See [[Epistemic Status and Claim Hygiene]].
