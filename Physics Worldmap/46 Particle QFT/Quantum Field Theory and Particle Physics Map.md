# Quantum Field Theory and Particle Physics Map

> [!abstract]
> Relativistic quantum fields, fundamental particles and interactions, symmetries, scattering, and physics beyond the Standard Model.

## Questions that organize the field

- What fields and symmetries constitute the Standard Model?
- How do particles and forces emerge from quantum fields?
- Which observations require physics beyond the Standard Model?
- How are ultraviolet and infrared descriptions connected?

## Working principles

- Relativistic locality, quantum mechanics, and symmetry constrain interactions.
- Observables are cross sections, decay rates, correlations, and infrared-safe quantities—not bare fields alone.
- Renormalization expresses scale dependence and effective description.
- New-physics claims require quantified backgrounds, systematics, global consistency, and trials factors.

## Canonical mathematical handles

- **Path integral:** $Z=\int\mathcal D\phi\,e^{iS[\phi]/\hbar}$ — Correlation functions arise from a sum over field configurations.
- **Gauge covariant derivative:** $D_\mu=\partial_\mu-igA_\mu^aT^a$ — Local symmetry introduces gauge connections.
- **Renormalization group:** $\mu\,dg/d\mu=\beta(g)$ — Couplings depend on observation scale.
- **Standard Model group:** $SU(3)_C\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$ — Gauge symmetry organizes known nongravitational interactions.

## Concept atlas

| Concept | Level | Status | One-line orientation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| [[Classical Fields]] | intermediate | established | A classical field assigns values to spacetime points and follows local equations derived from an action. |
| [[Scalar Field Theory]] | advanced | established | A scalar field is the simplest relativistic field and models order parameters, the Higgs sector, and effective degrees of freedom. |
| [[Klein Gordon Equation]] | advanced | established | A free relativistic scalar field obeys a wave equation with mass term. |
| [[Dirac Equation]] | advanced | established | A first-order relativistic equation describes spin-one-half fields and predicts antiparticles. |
| [[Quantum Fields and Particles]] | advanced | established | Particles are quantized excitations or asymptotic states of fields; interacting theories may have emergent quasiparticles and bound states. |
| [[Canonical Quantization of Fields]] | advanced | established | Classical fields and conjugate momenta become operator-valued distributions obeying equal-time (anti)commutation relations. |
| [[Path Integral Formulation]] | advanced | established | Quantum amplitudes and correlation functions are generated by integration over field configurations weighted by the action. |
| [[Quantum Field Correlation Functions]] | advanced | established | Time-ordered field correlations encode propagation, response, scattering amplitudes, and spectral information. |
| [[Feynman Diagrams]] | advanced | established | Diagrams bookkeep terms in perturbative expansions; internal lines are not literal observed particle trajectories. |
| [[Scattering Amplitudes]] | advanced | established | The S-matrix maps asymptotic incoming states to outgoing states; amplitudes yield cross sections and decay rates. |
| [[Renormalization]] | advanced | established | Bare parameters are replaced by scale-dependent measured quantities and counterterms so predictions remain finite order by order. |
| [[Renormalization Group in QFT]] | advanced | established | Changes of scale move couplings and operators along beta-function flows, revealing fixed points and effective descriptions. |
| [[Gauge Symmetry]] | advanced | established | Gauge redundancy expresses a local choice of internal frame; gauge-invariant quantities carry physical content. |
| [[Quantum Electrodynamics]] | advanced | established | QED is the renormalizable $U(1)$ gauge theory of charged fermions and photons, tested to extraordinary precision. |
| [[Quantum Chromodynamics]] | advanced | established | QCD is the $SU(3)$ gauge theory of quarks and gluons with asymptotic freedom and confinement. |
| [[Asymptotic Freedom]] | advanced | established | Non-Abelian gauge self-interactions make the QCD coupling decrease at high energy. |
| [[Confinement]] | advanced | active | Colored states are not observed in isolation at low energy; QCD forms hadrons and flux-tube-like interactions, though a complete analytic proof is absent. |
| [[Electroweak Theory]] | advanced | established | A chiral $SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$ gauge theory unifies weak and electromagnetic interactions after symmetry breaking. |
| [[Higgs Mechanism]] | advanced | established | A nonzero Higgs field vacuum expectation value gives masses to weak gauge bosons and fermions through gauge and Yukawa couplings. |
| [[Standard Model]] | advanced | established | The Standard Model combines QCD and electroweak theory with three fermion generations and a scalar Higgs sector. |
| [[Quarks and Hadrons]] | introductory | established | Six quark flavors form color-neutral mesons and baryons through QCD dynamics. |
| [[Leptons]] | introductory | established | Charged leptons and neutrinos form three generations with electroweak interactions and no color charge. |
| [[Neutrino Oscillations]] | advanced | established | Flavor states are superpositions of mass states, causing baseline- and energy-dependent flavor conversion. |
| [[CKM Mixing and CP Violation]] | advanced | established | Quark weak interactions mix mass eigenstates with a complex unitary matrix that violates CP. |
| [[Effective Field Theory]] | advanced | established | Heavy degrees of freedom generate symmetry-allowed local operators suppressed by a high scale at low energies. |
| [[Anomalies]] | advanced | established | A classical symmetry may fail after quantization; gauge anomalies must cancel for consistency while global anomalies have physical effects. |
| [[Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking]] | advanced | established | A symmetric action can have asymmetric stable states, producing collective modes and order parameters. |
| [[Goldstone Theorem]] | advanced | established | Spontaneously broken continuous global symmetries imply gapless modes under standard assumptions. |
| [[CPT and Spin Statistics]] | advanced | established | Local Lorentz-invariant unitary QFT obeys CPT symmetry, and integer or half-integer spin correlates with Bose or Fermi statistics. |
| [[Dark Matter Candidates]] | advanced | open | Nonluminous gravitating matter may be particles, fields, compact objects, or richer sectors constrained by astrophysical and laboratory data. |
| [[Axions and the Strong CP Problem]] | advanced | open | A dynamical pseudoscalar can relax the QCD CP angle and may constitute dark matter. |
| [[Supersymmetry]] | advanced | open | Supersymmetry relates bosons and fermions and can stabilize structures, but no superpartners have been established experimentally. |
| [[Grand Unification]] | advanced | open | Larger gauge groups may unify Standard Model interactions and imply relations such as charge quantization and proton decay. |
| [[Baryogenesis]] | advanced | open | The cosmic matter excess requires baryon-number violation, C and CP violation, and departure from equilibrium or alternatives to standard assumptions. |
| [[Hierarchy Problem]] | advanced | open | The electroweak scale is radiatively sensitive to much higher scales in simple formulations, motivating but not proving new physics. |
