# Scale Ladder

Orders of magnitude are a navigation axis, not rigid boundaries. The dominant degrees of freedom change as scale, energy, temperature, density, and observation time change.

| Approximate length | Representative structure | Useful descriptions |
|---:|---|---|
| $10^{-35}$ m | Planck length | unknown quantum gravity |
| $10^-19$–$10^-15$ m | electroweak to nucleon | [[Quantum Field Theory and Particle Physics Map]], [[Nuclear Physics Map]] |
| $10^-15$–$10^-10$ m | nuclei to atoms | [[Nuclear Physics Map]], [[Atomic Molecular and Optical Physics Map]] |
| $10^-10$–$10^-6$ m | molecules to cells | [[Condensed Matter Physics Map]], [[Soft Matter Map]], [[Biophysics Map]] |
| $10^{-6}$–$10^0$ m | cells to laboratory | continuum, statistical, biological, and classical descriptions |
| $10^0$–$10^7$ m | human to planet | [[Classical Mechanics Map]], [[Fluid Dynamics Map]], [[Geophysics and Climate Map]] |
| $10^7$–$10^13$ m | planets and systems | [[Astrophysics Map]], orbital and plasma physics |
| $10^{13}$–$10^{21}$ m | stellar to galactic | stellar, galactic, relativistic, and plasma astrophysics |
| $10^21$–$10^27$ m | galaxies to observable universe | [[Cosmology Map]] and relativistic structure formation |

## Crossovers worth understanding

- Quantum → classical: decoherence, coarse-graining, large action, limited observables.
- Microscopic → continuum: local equilibrium, scale separation, constitutive closure.
- Few-body → many-body: statistics, collective modes, phase transitions, emergence.
- Weak → strong gravity: post-Newtonian expansion, compactness, horizons.
- Collisionless → collisional: mean free path, collective relaxation, kinetic closure.
- Linear → nonlinear: perturbation growth, resonance, bifurcation, saturation.

## Scale-change questions

1. Which degrees of freedom are resolved?
2. Which are averaged or integrated out?
3. Which conservation laws and symmetries survive?
4. Which new effective parameters appear?
5. What is the expansion parameter?
6. Where does the approximation fail?
