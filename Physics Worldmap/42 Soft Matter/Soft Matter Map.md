# Soft Matter Map

> [!abstract]
> Polymers, colloids, membranes, liquid crystals, interfaces, granular media, and active materials dominated by thermal and geometric effects.

## Questions that organize the field

- How do entropy, interactions, geometry, and hydrodynamics organize mesoscopic matter?
- Why do weak forces produce strong nonlinear and history-dependent response?
- How do driven constituents create active phases unavailable in equilibrium?
- How can self-assembly be predicted and programmed?

## Working principles

- Compare interaction, elastic, gravitational, and active energies with $k_BT$.
- Hydrodynamic interactions are long-ranged and boundary-sensitive.
- Entropy can generate effective forces and order.
- Polydispersity, aging, and preparation protocol often matter.

## Canonical mathematical handles

- **Diffusion:** $\langle r^2\rangle=2dDt$ — Thermal motion spreads configurations.
- **Stokes-Einstein:** $D=k_BT/(6\pi\eta a)$ — Diffusion and drag are linked near equilibrium.
- **Capillary pressure:** $\Delta p=\gamma(1/R_1+1/R_2)$ — Surface tension couples curvature to pressure.
- **Polymer size:** $R\sim aN^\nu$ — Chain conformation follows universal scaling.

## Concept atlas

| Concept | Level | Status | One-line orientation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| [[Mesoscopic Scales]] | introductory | established | Soft matter lives between molecular and macroscopic scales where thermal energy competes with weak interactions and elasticity. |
| [[Colloids]] | introductory | established | Mesoscopic particles dispersed in a fluid exhibit Brownian motion, effective interactions, self-assembly, and glassiness. |
| [[Brownian Dynamics]] | intermediate | established | Overdamped stochastic equations model particle motion when inertia relaxes much faster than positions. |
| [[Polymers]] | introductory | established | Long flexible molecules have conformations controlled by connectivity, excluded volume, solvent quality, and entanglements. |
| [[Random Walk Polymer Model]] | introductory | established | An ideal chain maps to a random walk with root-mean-square size proportional to the square root of segment number. |
| [[Polymer Entanglement and Reptation]] | advanced | established | Long chains in a melt are constrained by neighbors and relax by snake-like motion along an effective tube. |
| [[Liquid Crystals]] | intermediate | established | Anisotropic molecules form phases with orientational or partial positional order and strong field response. |
| [[Membranes]] | advanced | established | Thin fluid sheets resist bending and stretching, fluctuate thermally, and organize biological compartments. |
| [[Surface Tension]] | introductory | established | Interfacial free energy per area drives droplets to reduce area and supports capillary waves. |
| [[Wetting and Contact Angles]] | intermediate | established | Interfacial energy balance determines whether a liquid spreads or forms a finite-angle droplet. |
| [[Capillarity]] | introductory | established | Surface tension and curvature create pressure differences and drive flow at small scales. |
| [[Self Assembly]] | intermediate | active | Components autonomously organize into structures favored by free-energy landscapes or driven kinetic pathways. |
| [[Depletion Forces]] | intermediate | established | Excluded-volume entropy of smaller particles can create an effective attraction between larger objects. |
| [[Gels]] | intermediate | established | A percolating network traps solvent and gives a soft material solid-like elasticity over long times. |
| [[Jamming]] | advanced | active | Dense disordered constituents acquire rigidity as free volume and rearrangement pathways vanish. |
| [[Rheology]] | intermediate | established | Rheology relates stress to deformation rate and history in complex materials. |
| [[Non Newtonian Fluids]] | introductory | established | Complex fluids may shear-thin, shear-thicken, yield, or remember deformation rather than having constant viscosity. |
| [[Active Matter]] | intermediate | active | Constituents consume energy locally to move or exert stress, creating collective states outside equilibrium. |
| [[Motility Induced Phase Separation]] | advanced | active | Persistent self-propelled particles can phase-separate through feedback between crowding and motility without attractive forces. |
